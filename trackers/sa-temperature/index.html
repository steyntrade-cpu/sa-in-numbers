What the data can’t tell us

  • These are modelled temperatures, not weather-station readings. ERA5 is a reanalysis: a weather model fitted to observations from satellites, weather stations and weather balloons, on a grid of about 25 km. It suits comparing years with each other; local station values will differ.
  • The latest weeks are preliminary. ECMWF publishes recent months as “ERA5T” and replaces them with final values two to three months later; the changes are usually a few hundredths of a degree.
  • Another dataset can rank years differently. Datasets agree on the big picture but can differ by a few tenths of a degree in a given year; a close ranking (within about 0.2 °C) is effectively a tie.
  • One national number hides big differences. The Lowveld, the Karoo and the coast can have very different days; this is the average over the whole country.
  • A year so far is not a full year. Ranks for the current year compare the same dates in every year, and can change as the year goes on.

Method

  • Temperature: daily mean air temperature at 2 m from ECMWF ERA5 hourly data, read from ECMWF’s analysis-ready (ARCO) store (Copernicus Climate Data Store), computed from hourly values over South African days. The national value is the average of all 1810 ERA5 grid cells (0.25°, about 25 km) inside South Africa (Lesotho and Eswatini excluded), weighted by cell area (cosine of latitude).
  • Why ERA5: until 22 November 2026 this page used NASA POWER. NASA POWER fills its latest two to three months from a different model (GEOS-IT) from the one behind every earlier year (MERRA-2). For 1 October to 15 November 2026 it read about 1.2 °C warmer than ERA5, while over 1981–2025 the two never differed by more than 0.4 °C for those dates (tests/check_era5_temperature.R). ERA5 is one system from start to finish.
  • Lines: a 7-day average (each day and the six before it), so the lines show weather spells rather than single days.
  • Average: the 1991–2020 mean for each day of the year (WMO standard period).
  • Ranks and anomalies: for the current year, 1 January to the latest date, compared with the same dates in every other year. Full-year values use complete calendar years.
  • Trend: a straight line fitted to the yearly averages of complete years (ordinary least squares), with its 95% confidence interval. Year-to-year swings are large, so the trend explains only part of the variation (r² = 0.35).
  • This year’s estimate: the 1991–2020 yearly average plus this year’s difference from normal so far. An average of January to November alone would leave out December and look too cool.
  • Warming since the 1980s: average of the last ten full years minus the average of 1981–1990 (+0.8°C).
